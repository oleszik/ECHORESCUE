# v0.15.2 multi-obstacle discovery and bounded repeated replanning

## Scope and trust boundary

v0.15.2 generalizes the v0.15.1 real Gazebo LiDAR pipeline from one discovery
to incremental, repeated mapping and replanning. Production control consumes
only typed MAVLink-derived telemetry, validated typed LiDAR observations, the
repository prior map and the accumulated EchoRescue map. Gazebo pose, contacts
and configured obstacle geometry remain inputs only to the independent
evaluator and owned-stack evidence harness.

Accepted scans require the existing frame, ENU, timestamp, ordering, range,
field-of-view, freshness and same-session pose contracts. Occupancy is never
cleared. Each effective observation batch records raw hit cells, conservative
unknown depth, newly inflated cells, pose, session and monotone map revision.
Duplicate endpoints consume no replan budget. Irrelevant changes are recorded
but do not replace a route.

[ADR 0023](adr/0023-bounded-repeated-replanning.md) defines mission/per-target
budgets, cooldown, planning timeout, consecutive-failure bound and generation
correlation. Every replacement calls `echorescue.planning.astar` directly from
the latest accepted pose and map, increments the route generation and installs
unique `gNNN-*` target IDs only after invalidating the obsolete active target.
Failure enters existing one-LAND-per-session recovery and still requires
`ON_GROUND` plus disarmed telemetry.

## Reproduction

After sourcing the pinned Gazebo/ArduPilot environment and built Jazzy workspace:

```bash
./scripts/run_planner_flight_integration.sh \
  --output artifacts/v0.15.2-baseline-v0.15.0.json smoke --timeout 300
./scripts/run_sensor_replanning_integration.sh \
  --output artifacts/v0.15.2-single-obstacle-regression.json smoke --timeout 300
./scripts/run_multi_obstacle_integration.sh \
  --output artifacts/v0.15.2-multi-obstacle-graphical.json multi --graphical --timeout 320
./scripts/run_multi_obstacle_integration.sh \
  --output artifacts/v0.15.2-multi-obstacle-headless.json multi --timeout 320
./scripts/run_multi_obstacle_integration.sh \
  --output artifacts/v0.15.2-replan-budget-exhaustion.json budget --timeout 320
./scripts/run_multi_obstacle_integration.sh \
  --output artifacts/v0.15.2-unreachable-later-discovery.json later_unreachable --timeout 320
```

## Accepted evidence

The unchanged v0.15.0 baseline and v0.15.1 single-obstacle regression passed.
The final headless multi-obstacle mission accepted three distinct effective
discovery batches at separated vehicle positions. Map revisions 1 and 2 each
invalidated the then-current route and produced route generations 2 and 3;
revision 3 was irrelevant and correctly skipped without consuming budget. The
drone reached the outbound goal, returned on the latest accumulated map, landed
and disarmed. Minimum obstacle and doorway surface clearance were 0.366 m and
0.340 m, with no prohibited contact.

The graphical run was visually inspected in the `v0.15.2_multi` Gazebo world:
the Iris, doorway, known blocker and multiple red unknown obstacles were visible
during the running reroute mission. It completed with two replans, three
irrelevant map updates safely skipped, no contact, 0.303 m obstacle clearance
and 0.342 m doorway clearance.

The budget scenario retained its first successful generation, rejected the
next relevant replacement with `replan budget exhausted`, then landed and
disarmed. The later-unreachable scenario likewise retained its first successful
replan, reported `no safe route after sensor discovery` on the later barrier,
and recovered without contact. Every report contains independent ROS events,
Gazebo trajectory/contact evidence, command ACK/telemetry correlations and
owned process, ROS-node and port cleanup.

## Limitations

- Obstacles are static; there is no dynamic-object tracking or occupancy decay.
- The prior map is authored and unknown space is not explored or cleared.
- Planning is cardinal 2D at a fixed altitude, not arbitrary 3D planning.
- This is one simulated drone with no hardware or multi-drone operation.
- Gazebo evaluation is Ground Truth acceptance evidence, never a control input.
- Simulator success is not evidence of real-aircraft readiness or safety.
