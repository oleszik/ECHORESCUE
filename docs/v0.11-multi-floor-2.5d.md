# v0.11 — Multi-floor / 2.5D

## Research question and result

v0.11 asks how autonomous multi-agent search-and-rescue changes when search,
Survivor confirmation, and coordination span several discretely connected
building floors. The implementation is deliberately 2.5D, not a 3D flight
simulator.

Across holdout seeds 50–99, both the size-matched flattened control and the
three-floor profile completed 50/50 missions with 100% Survivor Recall, 100%
return, and zero wall or drone collisions. Multi-floor missions averaged 64.52
steps, 204.00 path cost, 13.28 transitions, and 1.40 safely resolved transition
conflicts. No agent met the defined Floor Ping-Pong condition.

## Audit and compatibility boundary

The historical core assumes one `GridWorld`, `Position(x, y)`, four cardinal
neighbors, one width/height pair, a floorless Base, Survivors, dynamic
obstacles, task targets, range/LOS, replay coordinates, and one dashboard
canvas. Manhattan distance, A*, Frontier allocation, RTB, and energy all inherit
that assumption.

Reusable components are the independent 2D `GridWorld`, `CellState`, seeded
connected-map generation, deterministic A* discipline, energy accounting,
stable agent IDs, event/replay architecture, and collision invariants. Existing
2D internals remain unchanged. `--floors 1` and commands without `--floors`
still use the legacy simulator and replay schemas 1.5–2.4.

## Position and environment model

`GridPosition(floor, row, col)` is frozen, ordered, and typed. It exposes a
local legacy `Position(col, row)` only at the 2D floor boundary.
`MultiFloorEnvironment` owns:

- `floors: dict[int, GridWorld]`, allowing different dimensions;
- a Base position, normally on Floor 0;
- explicit `FloorTransition` objects.

Each generated benchmark floor is 13×9 with independent seeded walls and one
Survivor. Two stairwells connect Floor 0↔1 and Floor 1↔2, avoiding a single
connector dependency.

## Transition semantics

A transition stores source, destination, type, enabled state, directionality,
and traversal cost. Stairs default to bidirectional. Horizontal motion remains
N/S/E/W. No arbitrary `z` movement exists.

The frozen traversal cost is three simulated steps and three energy units.
During traversal the exit is reserved. Conflicting entry, opposite-direction
use, or an occupied exit is resolved deterministically. RTB has priority over
exploration; the loser moves to a known-free escape cell. If an exit remains
blocked, the transition is cancelled before entry and the other known
stairwell is planned. Agents never pass through each other.

## Planning, mapping, and sensing

Weighted A* adds horizontal edges of cost one and transition edges of cost
three. Its cross-floor heuristic is zero, so it remains admissible regardless
of connector geometry. Same-floor Manhattan distance is used only while source
and goal share a floor.

`MultiFloorKnowledge` keys every cell by `GridPosition`. UNKNOWN/FREE/OCCUPIED
semantics remain unchanged. Transition topology and endpoints are initially
known; all other floor occupancy is unknown. Sensors reveal only the current
floor. Survivors and hypotheses at equal row/column on different floors remain
different identities. There is no cross-floor Visual, Thermal, Smoke, wall, or
dynamic-obstacle leakage.

## Frontier and floor allocation

Frontiers are detected per floor and combined into globally ordered candidates.
Candidate cost is weighted path cost plus the frozen same-floor congestion term
`4 × agents already assigned to that floor`. No agent-to-floor table exists.

Development showed that the transition cost itself prevented ordinary
oscillation. A semantic sticky rule was added for a genuine edge case: after an
agent has entered another floor, it finishes available local Frontiers before
switching again. This removed opportunistic Floor Ping-Pong without a new
numeric threshold or role.

## RTB, energy, failures, and obstacles

Return paths use the same weighted graph and include every required stair.
Energy reserve checks include movement, sensing, transition cost, and reserve.
An agent failing during a transition remains on its actual source cell; the
pending transition is cancelled, the cell becomes unavailable to planning,
and its floor-aware exploration objective is deterministically reassigned.

In the seed-0 regression, Drone 1 fails at step 5, Drone 3 takes the released
work, all Survivors are confirmed, all three operational agents return, and
collisions remain zero. Dynamic-obstacle injection accepts
`FLOOR:ROW,COL:STEP`; legacy `X,Y:STEP` remains unchanged for 2D missions.

## Communication

The main benchmark deliberately uses ideal/shared communication to isolate
floor planning. The constrained regression uses normal same-floor Manhattan
range and permits a cross-floor link only between the two endpoints of the
same stair transition. This is an explicit abstraction, not through-floor RF.
Roles remain GENERALIST/SCOUT/VERIFIER/RELAY; floor is not a role. Predictive
Multi-Relay is not part of the v0.11 main benchmark.

## Replay, dashboard, and telemetry

Replay schema 2.5 stores floor-aware Base, positions, paths, targets,
Survivors, per-floor maps, transitions, and active Floor. Legacy 2.x remains
accepted. Events include transition start/completion/conflict, target
assignment, floor entry, exploration completion, and floor-task reassignment.

The dashboard provides Floor tabs through a selector, a live per-floor agent
overview, Floor badges on cards and targets, stair markers, per-floor trails,
and only the selected 2D occupancy map. It intentionally avoids pseudo-3D.

## Development and frozen parameters

Seeds 0–9 were used before holdout. Final means were 63.70 mission steps,
195.80 path cost, 13.40 transitions, 0.90 conflicts, and a 0.459674 Floor
balance score. The 2/4/8-agent five-seed check completed every mission:

| Agents | Success/Recall/Return | Mean duration | Mean path cost | Mean transitions |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 100% / 100% / 100% | 81.20 | 154.40 | 6.00 |
| 4 | 100% / 100% / 100% | 64.00 | 200.00 | 14.00 |
| 8 | 100% / 100% / 100% | 63.80 | 250.00 | 15.60 |

Frozen values are transition/path cost 3, transition energy 3, two stairwells
per adjacent-floor pair, congestion penalty 4, Visual sensing, and
ideal/shared communication. The sticky local-work rule has no tuned parameter.

## Holdout and flattened control

The holdout uses seeds 50–99, four agents, three floors, one Survivor per
floor, Visual sensing, Noise/Smoke/Dynamic Obstacles/Failures/Relay off, and
ideal/shared communication.

The flattened control is 37×9 (333 cells) against three 13×9 floors (351 total
cells), with three Survivors and four agents. It approximately controls search
space size, but boundary geometry differs. The paired delta therefore cannot
be interpreted as a perfectly isolated causal transition effect.

Values are `mean / population std / median / min / max`:

| Metric | Flattened control | Three floors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission duration | 89.78/5.60/90/80/104 | 64.52/7.02/63/52/85 |
| Total path cost | 326.88/16.50/325/298/366 | 204.00/16.95/202/174/242 |
| Transition count | — | 13.28/1.59/14/8/16 |
| Transition conflicts | — | 1.40/3.22/.5/0/21 |
| First new floor | — | 6/0/6/6/6 |
| Vertical transition overhead | — | 26.56/3.18/28/16/32 |
| Floor work balance | — | .503906/.098976/.498944/.283582/.740740 |

Both profiles have mission success, Recall, and return rates of 1.0 and zero
collisions. The paired Multi-floor-minus-flattened means are −25.26 duration
steps and −122.88 path cost. This does not mean stairs make search intrinsically
faster: separate compact floors reduce long horizontal travel and boundary
geometry is not identical. Explicit vertical overhead remains +26.56 weighted
steps per mission.

Mean explored coverage was approximately 98% on every floor. The committed artifact
contains per-seed rows and mean/population-std/median/min/max distributions for
coverage, path cost, and work share on Floors 0, 1, and 2:
[`benchmarks/multi_floor_50_holdout_seeds.json`](../benchmarks/multi_floor_50_holdout_seeds.json).

## Per-seed findings

- Seed 68 has the most transition conflicts (21), 16 transitions, duration 85,
  good balance 0.608109, full Recall/return, and zero collisions.
- Seed 63 also lasts 85 steps, with seven conflicts and balance 0.352273.
- Seed 73 has the weakest balance (0.283582): work shares are 59.82%, 23.21%,
  and 16.96%, yet it finishes in 56 steps with no conflict or Recall loss.
- Every mission first enters a new floor at step 6.
- No seed loses Recall, misses RTB, times out, collides, or meets the defined
  Ping-Pong condition.
- More agents were not worse in the development scaling aggregate: 8-agent
  duration 63.80 versus 4-agent 64.00, although total path cost rises.

[`replays/seed_68_multi_floor.json`](../replays/seed_68_multi_floor.json) is the

## Limitations and next research direction

- This is discrete 2.5D, not free 3D flight or physical stair traversal.
- Transition locations are known from mission start.
- Shared knowledge and centralized allocation are deliberate benchmark choices.
- Floor shapes and the flattened control have different boundary geometry.
- Cross-floor constrained communication is a stair-endpoint abstraction.
- Collapse, dynamic stair blockage, elevators, voxels, realistic RF, ROS 2,
  MAVLink, and hardware are out of scope.

v0.11 ends the stored core roadmap. A later roadmap may evaluate distributed
coordination, realistic RF, SITL/hardware bridges, richer visualization, or
sensor fusion, but none is implemented here.
